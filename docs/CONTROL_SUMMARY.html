Control laws

The half-pipe controller follows a staged climb, departure, roll and landing sequence. It turns errors into force/torque requests, then distributes those requests to the jets that actually exist. This page gives the intuition; the detailed control document provides equations, timing, scaling and exact success criteria.

1. One maneuver, six stages

Departure starts when the actual seal is lost above the height threshold. Later stages use elapsed time and physical contact/state checks. The controller saves the launch pose as a reference; it never teleports the vehicle to the next stage.

This is the continuous half-pipe example. Basic ceiling, downforce and lift use scheduled fan/thrust commands instead of this flight controller.

2. Why the roll loses height

The roll is around the vehicle's front-to-rear axis, keeping its launch forward direction. There are no top jets. While inverted, bottom-jet force points downward in the world frame. Firing one side more strongly creates roll torque, but also adds net force; it cannot independently support weight.

The controller therefore allows altitude loss during inversion, then requests upward braking once almost upright. Success depends on having enough height, remaining gas, thrust and attitude authority to recover before touching the floor.

In the nozzle sketch, arrows show force on the vehicle, not exhaust. The four circle-and-dot symbols represent bottom-jet force toward body +z, out of the top-view page. The front side jets push laterally; the rear jet pushes forward.

3. Feedback: correct the error and slow the motion

P — correction: a position or orientation error asks for motion back toward the target.

D — damping: velocity feedback brakes that motion so the vehicle does not simply overshoot. During position tracking, the controller also adds gravity compensation. The full-size preset adds reference-acceleration feed-forward; the planned roll includes angular-rate/acceleration references.

For recovery, the horizontal target moves toward the landing area while a height target requests recovery from the fall. The descent target then lowers smoothly. The recovery height target can step upward; the vehicle's actual position stays continuous.

To move sideways or backward, the controller mainly tilts the bottom jets so their force has a horizontal component. It is not using an invisible lateral force. Position and attitude feedback are coupled: until the body has tilted, the requested world force may not be achieved.

4. Seven jets do not provide seven independent controls

Requested actionHow it is producedCoupling or limit
Common liftFour underside jets togetherForce only toward body +z; tank pressure limits output
Roll / pitchDifferent underside-jet strengthsMay require extra common thrust; clipping changes the result
YawOne of the two front lateral jetsAlso produces lateral force
Forward departureRear jetCannot reverse its force
Horizontal recoveryTilt the body and bottom-jet forceRequires attitude tracking and time

The layout's instantaneous force/torque matrix has rank five. In particular, front lateral force and yaw torque are coupled. A planned trajectory can use changing attitude over time, but the controller cannot independently command every force and torque at the same instant.

The allocator distributes roll/pitch requests among the four bottom jets, adds a common positive bias if a request would otherwise need negative thrust, and clips each jet to its current pressure-dependent limit. This can produce more net force than requested, or too little torque when saturated. allocation_error records the predicted mismatch; its mixed force/torque norm has no single unit.

5. What counts as success?

The sequence records first and repeated touchdown speeds, watches gas and timeout limits, and requires floor contact, nearly upright attitude and low linear speed before declaring landed. Touchdown speed includes horizontal motion, not just vertical fall speed. A stage label alone is insufficient: the CLI rejects a run with a failure even if it later reaches the floor.

This is a PD controller tuned for the supplied profiles, with direct access to simulation state. It has no integral term or sensor estimator. Generic hover control, arbitrary nozzle layouts, disturbed initial conditions and flight-to-ceiling capture require further design and validation.
