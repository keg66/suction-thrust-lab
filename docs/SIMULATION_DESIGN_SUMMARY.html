Simulation model design

The vehicle moves because the simulator applies forces and integrates motion. The fan changes contact loading, wheels provide traction, and gas jets push and rotate the vehicle. This page explains that structure without the implementation equations; the detailed model design covers the numerical methods and code.

1. What is simulated?

MuJoCo calculates where the vehicle goes next. The custom Python code calculates fan pressure, gas use and applied loads. A target position is a request for the controller, not a position assigned to the vehicle.

The basic examples have a freely moving body with fixed wheel shapes. The half-pipe examples add four rotating, independently driven wheels. The decorative body, track edges and landing marker do not create extra forces.

2. Suction presses the tires onto a nearby surface

The fan removes air from a small volume under the skirt. Lower pressure there creates an attraction toward the surface:

Suction force = pressure difference × effective area.

SurfaceWhat the force does
FloorAdds to the vehicle's weight and increases tire loading
CeilingPulls upward; suction must exceed weight to maintain a positive contact load
WallPresses the tires against the wall; tire friction/drive must support weight along it

For the basic 120 g vehicle and 0.01 m² area, about 118 Pa balances weight alone. It is a useful force check, not a complete climbing or ceiling-retention design.

The wall sketch explains the force balance, not an implemented wall-driving demonstration. Current flat-ceiling behavior is initialized attachment and release; generic wall/ceiling driving is still planned.

3. A fan only works with a usable seal

A large gap lets more air in. A fan's flow also falls as pressure difference rises: maximum flow and maximum pressure cannot be delivered together. The model includes this pressure–flow tradeoff, fan startup delay and finite battery use.

Flat surfaces are checked at all four skirt corners. The curved course uses rays at the corners and center; losing a required hit breaks the seal. Within the curved skirt's allowed travel, the current model assumes a constant residual leak. It does not gradually model skirt deformation. A distant wall or ceiling never attracts the vehicle.

4. Jets use finite gas and can also rotate the body

Thrust = gas mass flow × effective exhaust velocity.

A jet pushes opposite its exhaust direction. If its force acts away from the chassis center, it also creates a turning moment. The simulator applies force at the actual configured nozzle location, allowing MuJoCo to calculate the resulting motion.

All jets share a finite tank. Using gas lowers pressure and available thrust; no usable gas means no thrust. The half-pipe profiles also reduce vehicle mass/inertia as gas is consumed. Basic examples keep their mass fixed as a simpler approximation.

Battery use, gas use and exhaust kinetic energy are logged. They are useful resource indicators, not a complete thermodynamic or electrical model.

5. How to interpret the result

Implemented approximationWhat it does not establish
Primitive collision shapes and estimated inertiaAccurate dynamics of a manufactured vehicle
Lumped pressure, leakage and fan curveDetailed flow around a real skirt
Constant-temperature ideal gas and effective exhaust velocityReal high-pressure tank/nozzle performance
Contact/friction and driven wheelsMeasured tire or suspension behavior
Miniature and experimental full-size presetsA controlled scale-only comparison or practical full-size feasibility

The full-size profile uses separately assumed hardware and control settings. In particular, its high tank pressure needs real-gas and thermal validation. A completed trajectory shows what the mathematical model can do under those assumptions.
